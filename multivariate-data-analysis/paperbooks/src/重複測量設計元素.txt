=== Slide 31 ===
Summary Statistics Approach
The most elementary method to reduce a vector of repeated measurements to a single summary statistics.
Recall that Student(1908) reduce the vector with two dimensions into one single score.
The model can be represented as di=B0+ei, where di is the difference between pre-test and post test for subject I
Wishart(1938) elaborated with a study of three different levels of protein content effects on three groups of bacon pigs over 16 weeks where growth rate was determined based on the weight gain

=== Slide 32 ===
Special case of SS approach: Paired T-Test
Paired T-Test is a special case of summary statistics. Paired T-Test also called the dependent sample t-test, matched-paired t test.
Compare the means of two related groups to see if there are any statistically differences between these means.
What does it mean by “related”?
Natural pairs: twins
Matched pairs: matched control group method
Repeated measures: pre-post design

=== Slide 33 ===
Explore paired T-Test
Recall that in independent sample T-Test,
H0: μ1 = μ2  v.s.  H1: μ1 ≠ μ2
However, the natural dependency of these two groups violates the assumption of the independent sample T-Test.
In paired T-Test, we examine H0: μ1 − μ2 = 0
Paired T-Test examines the differences between means when subjects are measured with the same dv but two different conditions.
Paired T-Test also examines the change between means when subjects are measured with the same dv but two different timings.

ANCOVA Model

yij is the jth response measured at treatment level I
μ + αi we can consider this  as treatment i mean
β is the slope of the two lines
xij is the jth value of the covariate at treatment level i

=== Slide 61 ===
Steps for analyzing ANCOVA
yij = μ + αi + β(xij) + αiβ(xij) + εij
1. Analyze the full model, including the covariate and the interaction between the covariate and the treatment effect.
2. Test the interaction first. If it is significant, stop. It indicates you have multiple lines with multiple slopes. If it is not significant, it indicates you have multiple lines with the same slope. Continue to the next step.
3. Test the covariate. If it is significant, stop. It indicates you are looking at multiple lines with parallel significant slopes. If it is not significant, it indicates you have multiple means, not multiple lines. Continue to the next step.
4. Conduct a basic ANOVA analysis.
