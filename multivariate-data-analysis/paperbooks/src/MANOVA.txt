=== Slide 8 ===
In ANOVA
The model: Y=X1+X2+X3+X3+..+Xn
F statistic = MSM / MSR
F statistic = the ratio of total amount of variation that needs to be explained by:
MSM = systematic variation :variance given that all observations come from single distribution
MSR = residual variation : variance of each condition separately
If F < 1,  MSR > MSM
If F > 1,  MSR < MSM

=== Slide 12 ===
Why to use MANOVA
You do a MANOVA instead of a series of one-at-a-time ANOVAs for two main reasons
to reduce the experiment-wise level of Type I error.
None of the individual ANOVAs may produce a significant main effect on the response, but in combination they might, which suggests that the variables are more meaningful taken together than considered separately
If the number of DV is relatively low(5 or fewer), the statistical power of the MANOVA  equals or exceed that obtained with a single ANOVA
–MANOVA takes into account the inter-correlations among the response Variables

=== Slide 13 ===
Sample Size Requirement
The sample size requirements for MANOVA relate to individual group sizes and not the total sample.
As a bare minimum, the sample in each group must be greater than the number of DV’s
As a practical guide, a recommended minimum cell size is 20
As the number of DV increases, the sample size required increases. For example, required sample sizes increase by 50 percent as the number of DV goes from two to six

=== Slide 16 ===
MANOVA: Test Statistics
Wilk’s Lambda (Λ) was the first MANOVA test statistic developed and is very important for several multivariate procedures in addition to MANOVA.
Wilk’s Lambda (Λ) is the error sum of squares (E) divided by the sum of the effect sum of squares (H) and the error sum of squares. i.e., random variation/systematic variation.
The quantity (1 - Λ) is often interpreted as the proportion of variance in the dependent variables explained by the model effect. However, this quantity is not unbiased and can be quite misleading in small samples.
Λ is approximately chi-square distributed
Even though there are tables of critical values for Λ, but they are virtually never used

=== Slide 17 ===
MANOVA: assumptions
Independence: Observations must be independent
The set of dependent variables must follow a multivariate normal distribution (any linear combination of the DV follow a normal distribution).
i.e.,If all DV’s are multivariate normal collectively, then Each DV has a univariate normal dist. However, converse is not necessarily true.
Equality of Variance-Covariance matrix: Variance-covariance matrices must be equal for all the treatment groups
